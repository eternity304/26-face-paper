\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem_formulation}

Consider two images $\mathbf{I}_1$ and $\mathbf{I}_2$ of the same face, taken
from arbitrary viewpoint and time. Let $\mathbf{X}_t[p]\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$
denote the 3D position of facial point $p$
in observation $t$. We define its scene flow from $\mathbf{I}_1$ to
$\mathbf{I}_2$ as the $\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{1\rightarrow2}[p]$ that satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{X}}_2[p]
    = \mathbf{X}_1[p]+\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{1\rightarrow2}[p].
    \label{eq:scene_flow}
\end{equation}
All 3D points are expressed in a common coordinate frame; the definition is
therefore independent of whether the change between the observations is caused
by facial motion or camera motion.

Let $\mathbf{u}_1[p]=(i_1,j_1)$ and $z_1[p]$ be the image coordinate and depth
of $p$ in $\mathbf{I}_1$. Instead of regressing the 3D displacement directly,
we estimate its optical-flow component $\boldsymbol{\mu}[p]$ and depth change
$\Delta z[p]$, which together determine the point in $\mathbf{I}_2$ as
\begin{align}
    \hat{\mathbf{u}}_2[p]
        &= \mathbf{u}_1[p]+\boldsymbol{\mu}[p], \\
    \hat z_2[p]
        &= z_1[p]+\Delta z[p], \\
    \hat{\mathbf{X}}_2[p]
        &= \pi_2^{-1}\!\left(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_2[p],\hat z_2[p]\right),
    \label{eq:flow_unprojection}
\end{align}
where $\pi_2^{-1}$ denotes unprojection under the camera of
$\mathbf{I}_2$. Thus, estimating scene flow reduces to estimating a 2D optical
flow and a one-dimensional depth change for every facial point.

For our method specifically, we fix the canonical face mesh as the first
observation.
Each canonical point has a fixed 3D position, depth $z_p^c$, and UV coordinate,
and the model predicts $\boldsymbol{\mu}[p]$ and $\Delta z[p]$ that parametrize
the flow field from the canonical face mesh to the face in the input image.


\subsection{Network Architecture}
\label{sec:network_architecture}

Given one or more face images of a subject, our network recovers the 3D
positions of dense facial landmarks defined by a fixed canonical mesh topology.
It predicts both the observed expression geometry and the expression-neutral
identity geometry while preserving this vertex correspondence.
To this end, we first encode the inputs with a mutliview encoder that follows with
a learnt feedforward layer that lifts the images features into 3D feature volumes.
Then, two recurrent flow branches iteratively refine the 3D flow components
for each point on the canonical face before recovering the corresponding 3D expression
and identity meshes.

\paragraph{Multi-View Geometry 3D Encoding.}
\label{sec:multiview_encoder}
We use a pretrained 3D foundational encoder, following recent general-purpose reconstruction
models~\cite{wang2026vggtomega,depthanything3,pi3}. It consist of transformer layers with two type
of attention ordered in alternate, processing both per-view, and cross-view information.
This allows the model to take a set $\mathcal{I}=\{\mathbf{I}^i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ of face image
with $N\geq1$ as input, supporting both monocular and multiview inferences. For each of image latent features $\mathbf{X^i}$ produced,
a learned decoder transform them into an 3D voxel feature grid $\mathbf{F}^i\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times V\times U\times D}$.
Here $V$ and $U$ index the $xy$ image-plane position and $D$ indexes depth.

\paragraph{UV-3D Flow}
We extend the recurrent all-pairs matching strategy of
RAFT~\cite{teed2020raft} from a 2D image grid to a canonical UV domain and a 3D
feature volume. We follow FlowFace's embedding architecture and parametrizes our flow map using a
2D UV map that parametrizes per vertex scene flow. Its corresponding is a learned network module consisting of a 32 channel texture pyramid
under 3 levels of coarse to fine resolution. Further, RAFT's hidden state $h_0$, positionl embedding $u_0$, context $c$ are initialized in the
same manner.

The RAFT network is designed to compute correlation between two images using a 4D
correlttion volume; we extend this design to computing correlation between the 2D UV map representing scene flow and
the 3D voxel feature, outputing a 5D correlation volume $\mathbf{C}$. Then, for each iteration $k$ during inference,
the model performs a look up in the correlation volume base on the current follow estimation $S^{i}_{k-1}$.
Using the look up, previous flow $S^{i}_{k-1}$, context $c$, it refine output the new flow estimate $S^{i}_{k}$.
Throughout, the superscript $i$ indexes an input image and the subscript $k$
indexes a recurrent refinement iteration.
Formally, the recurrent update module $f_\theta$ has form:

\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{S}^{i}_{k}, \mathbf{h}^{i}_{k}
    = f_\theta(\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{X}^{i},\mathbf{F}^{i}),
    \mathbf{S}^{i}_{k-1}, \mathbf{c}, \mathbf{h}^{i}_{k-1})
\end{equation}



\subsection{Mesh Reconstruction}
\label{sec:metric_depth}

After $K$ recurrent iterations, the final flow $\mathbf{S}^{i}_{K}$ is
decoded into an optical flow $\boldsymbol{\mu}^{i}[p]$ and a relative depth
change $\Delta z^{i}[p]$ for every canonical UV coordinate $p$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{S}^{i}_{K}[p]
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \boldsymbol{\mu}^{i}[p] \\
        \Delta z^{i}[p]
    \end{bmatrix}.
    \label{eq:decoded_flow}
\end{equation}
Let $\mathbf{u}^{c}[p]$ and $z^{c}[p]$ denote the fixed UV coordinate and
relative depth of point $p$ on the canonical mesh. Following
\cref{sec:problem_formulation}, we add the predicted flow components to their
canonical coordinates:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathbf{u}}^{i}[p]
        = \mathbf{u}^{c}[p]+\boldsymbol{\mu}^{i}[p],
    \qquad
    \tilde z^{i}[p]
        = z^{c}[p]+\Delta z^{i}[p].
    \label{eq:canonical_flow_decode}
\end{equation}
This yields the image position and relative depth of every output vertex at relative scale/

Metric-depth recovery is out of scope of our model. We obtain a near-depth
estimate $z_{\mathrm{near}}^{i}$ from an off-the-shelf depth predictor and use
it to define an adaptive metric interval. Given the image width
$W_{\mathrm{img}}$, focal length $f_x^{i}$, a fixed extent factor $\rho$, and
margin $m$, the interval is
\begin{align}
    z_{\min}^{i}
        &= z_{\mathrm{near}}^{i}-m, \\
    z_{\max}^{i}
        &= z_{\mathrm{near}}^{i}
        \left(1+\rho\frac{W_{\mathrm{img}}}{f_x^{i}}\right)+m.
    \label{eq:depth_bounds}
\end{align}
The ratio $W_{\mathrm{img}}/f_x^{i}$ is the angular width of the face crop.
Thus, the interval extent adapts to both camera field of view and subject
distance without requiring a separately predicted far bound.

We map the decoded relative depth to a normalized coordinate and scale it to
this interval:
\begin{equation}
    \hat z^{i}[p]
    = z_{\min}^{i}
      +\tilde z^{i}[p](z_{\max}^{i}-z_{\min}^{i}).
    \label{eq:relative_to_metric}
\end{equation}

Finally, we unproject the predicted image position and metric depth to recover
the 3D mesh vertex:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{X}}^{i}[p]
    = \pi^{-1}( \tilde u^{i}[p], \hat z^{i}[p])
    \label{eq:unprojection}
\end{equation}


\subsection{Expression and Neutral Geometry Branches}
\label{sec:dual_branch}

A face observation contains both subject-specific identity geometry and
transient expression deformation. We recover both using two recurrent UV--3D
flow branches. Let $b\in\{\mathrm{expr},\mathrm{neu}\}$ denote a branch and
$\mathbf{S}^{i,b}_{k}$ its flow estimate for input image $i$ after iteration
$k$. The expression branch predicts $\mathbf{S}^{i,\mathrm{expr}}_{K}$, which
maps the canonical mesh to the observed facial geometry. The neutral branch
predicts $\mathbf{S}^{i,\mathrm{neu}}_{K}$, which maps the same canonical mesh
to the expression-neutral geometry of the subject.

The two branches share the multiview encoder, 3D voxel decoder, and learned
canonical UV initialization. Each branch has an independent motion encoder,
recurrent update block, and flow head, allowing it to query the correlation
volume along a branch-specific flow trajectory. We apply the reconstruction
procedure in \cref{sec:metric_depth} independently to each final flow to obtain
the expression mesh $\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{i,\mathrm{expr}}$ and neutral identity
mesh $\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{i,\mathrm{neu}}$ in a single forward pass.


\subsection{Training Objectives}
\label{sec:training_objectives}

We supervise both branches using ground truth meshes with the canonical
topology. For branch $b\in\{\mathrm{expr},\mathrm{neu}\}$, let
$\mathbf{X}^{i,b,*}[p]$ be the ground-truth position of vertex $p$ for input
image $i$. The expression target is the observed facial geometry, while the
neutral target is the expression-neutral geometry of the same subject. We
project each target using the camera intrinsics $\mathbf{K}^{i}$ to obtain its
normalized image position $\mathbf{u}^{i,b,*}[p]$ and metric depth
$z^{i,b,*}[p]$.

Following \cref{sec:metric_depth}, we reconstruct the image position
$\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{i,b}[p]$ and metric depth $\hat z_{k}^{i,b}[p]$ at
every recurrent iteration. We supervise these coordinates with
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{coord},k}^{i,b}
        = \frac{1}{N_v}\sum_{p=1}^{N_v} w_p \big[&
        \|\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{i,b}[p]
        -\mathbf{u}^{i,b,*}[p]\|_2^2 \\
        &+ \lambda_z
        (\hat z_{k}^{i,b}[p]-z^{i,b,*}[p])^2 \big].
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:coordinate_loss}
\end{equation}
where $N_v$ is the number of vertices, $w_p$ is a predefined vertex weight,
and $\lambda_z$ balances image-plane and depth errors.

To preserve local surface orientation, we unproject the prediction and compute
per-vertex normals using the fixed mesh topology. We minimize their cosine
distance to the ground-truth normals:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{normal},k}^{i,b}
    =
    \frac{
        \sum_{p=1}^{N_v} w_p
        \left(1-
        \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{k}^{i,b}[p]\cdot
        \mathbf{n}^{i,b,*}[p]\right)
    }{
        \sum_{p=1}^{N_v}w_p
    }.
    \label{eq:normal_loss}
\end{equation}

We supervise all recurrent predictions with exponentially increasing weight
toward the final iteration. Defining $\lambda_{\mathrm{expr}}=1$, the complete
dual-branch objective is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}
    =
    \sum_{b\in\{\mathrm{expr},\mathrm{neu}\}}\lambda_b
    \sum_{k=1}^{K}\gamma^{K-k}
    \left(
        \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{coord},k}^{i,b}
        +\lambda_n\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{normal},k}^{i,b}
    \right),
    \label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
averaged over all input images in the batch. Here, $\gamma$ is the recurrent
decay factor, $\lambda_n$ weights normal consistency, and
$\lambda_{\mathrm{neu}}$ controls the neutral branch.

In our implementation, we use $K=4$, $\gamma=0.6$, $\lambda_z=0.5$,
$\lambda_n=0.1$, and $\lambda_{\mathrm{neu}}=1$. Training uses direct
registered 3D supervision and requires neither photometric inverse rendering
nor test-time optimization.
